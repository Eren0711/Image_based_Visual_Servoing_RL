ASYMMETRIC AGILITY ABLATION — INTERPRETATION
============================================================

Setup: HardNet-D (lambda=0.05) locked policy, the SAME checkpoint
used in the standard evader evaluation (Claim A). 100 seeds (0-99)
per maneuver mode, identical across multipliers, deterministic
policy. Only the interceptor's a_max, f_max, and omega_max are
scaled; the target is untouched (verified: target.a_max stays
10.0 m/s^2 while interceptor.a_max reaches 30.0 at 3.0x).

Steady-turn success by multiplier (%):
  1.0x=0  1.2x=0  1.5x=0  2.0x=0  3.0x=0

1) First multiplier with >30% steady-turn success: NONE (no multiplier reached 30%)

2) Is mean success monotonic in agility? NO
   Mean success across modes per multiplier: 1.0x=24.2%, 1.2x=25.6%, 1.5x=23.6%, 2.0x=14.0%, 3.0x=0.0%
   -> Non-monotonic: extra agility does not help and can hurt,
      consistent with the trained policy being driven out of
      distribution when its control gains are amplified.

3) Does closure improve with agility on steady turns even when
   success does not?
   mean closure rate (m/s, neg=closing): 1.0x=-1.80, 1.2x=-1.80, 1.5x=-1.37, 2.0x=-0.44, 3.0x=0.16
   mean min-range (m): 1.0x=13.7, 1.2x=13.8, 1.5x=14.8, 2.0x=15.9, 3.0x=16.9
   closure improves with agility: NO; closest-approach improves: NO

4) CONCLUSION: the failure is GUIDANCE-LIMITED.

5) RECOMMENDED NEXT EXPERIMENT: Proceed to the lead-pursuit / predictive-guidance experiment. Even 2.0x interceptor agility does not lift turning interception above 10%, so simply giving the existing guidance more muscle is not the fix; the guidance law itself must anticipate the turn.

------------------------------------------------------------
CONFOUNDS FLAGGED (not fixed, per task spec):
- Normalized action space: actions are in [-1,1] and scaled by
  a_max, so raising the limits also amplifies the TRAINED policy's
  effective control gains. The policy was calibrated at 1.0x, so
  high multipliers push it out of distribution (e.g. cruise itself
  can degrade). A clean agility-limit test would require RETRAINING
  at each multiplier; this zero-shot ablation cannot fully separate
  'more agility' from 'amplified, miscalibrated commands'.
- v_max, max_pitch, and max_roll are NOT scaled. Top speed stays
  capped (interceptor already out-cruises the ~12 m/s target), and
  achievable lateral acceleration from tilting remains bounded by
  the fixed attitude limits, partially gating the benefit of larger
  a_max/f_max.
- Observation space is unchanged: body velocity is normalized by the
  unscaled v_max and attitude by the unscaled max_pitch/max_roll, so
  those observation components can saturate at high multipliers,
  changing their effective meaning to the policy.
- Success radius for reference: d_success = 2 m.
